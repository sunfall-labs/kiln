## What this cannot see

- **A function is only ever `function`.** The description both engines print is `typeof` plus, for a primitive, its value. It deliberately does not read `.name` or `.length` off the value to tell two functions apart, because those are members this very inventory finds missing on dozens of receivers --- the scaffolding would report its own gap and call it the member's. Two different functions therefore compare equal, and so do two different objects. A member that is present, callable, and *wrong* is outside this inventory; `conformance/native` is where that is caught.
- **One level of nesting.** Globals, their statics, their prototypes, the object each of those inherits from, and the `node:` namespaces. A namespace inside a namespace (`path.posix`) is measured as one member, not walked.
- **Symbol-keyed members only where they are well known.** A symbol is reported as `@@name` when it is a property of `Symbol`; a registered or private symbol has no spelling this probe can put to the other engine.
- **57 receivers cannot describe themselves natively.** `Object.getOwnPropertyNames` answers on the built-ins ADR 0043 put on the shared table, on the prototypes and on the namespaces, and refuses elsewhere. On those receivers an *invented* member --- one native publishes and the interpreter does not --- is invisible, because nothing would have thought to ask for it by name.
- **Instances are not probed.** Everything here is read off a constructor, a prototype or a namespace. A member that resolves differently on a live receiver than on the prototype it came from is not something this method can reach.
