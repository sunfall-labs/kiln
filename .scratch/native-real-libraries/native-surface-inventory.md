A receiver key is the path the walk took. `Array.prototype` is what it says; a trailing `^` means *the object this one inherits from*, which is the only way the shared prototypes get inventoried --- every typed array's own prototype has two members and the thirty-five that matter live one link up, on an object no name on the global object reaches. `node:` receivers were probed with `--node`; everything else without it.

`(itself)` and `(itself).name` are rows about the receiver rather than about a member of it, and there are 263 of them. They exist because the walk cannot ask them: it enumerates members, so a receiver that is a *function* in one engine and an *object* in the other agrees on every member it has and diverges on what it is. `typeof require("node:module") === "function"` is a real feature test, and it read two ways in the two engines for months with every member of the module agreeing. A `.name` row appears only where one of the engines answers a name; a receiver both engines agree has none is not a measurement of anything.

## What this cannot see

- **A function is only ever `function`.** The description both engines print is `typeof` plus, for a primitive, its value. It deliberately does not read `.name` or `.length` off the value to tell two functions apart, because those are members this very inventory finds missing on dozens of receivers --- the scaffolding would report its own gap and call it the member's. Two different functions therefore compare equal, and so do two different objects. A member that is present, callable, and *wrong* is outside this inventory; `conformance/native` is where that is caught. The `(itself).name` row is the one exception, and it is narrow: a receiver's *own* name is read, because a receiver is named for what it is rather than for a member it publishes.
- **One level of nesting.** Globals, their statics, their prototypes, the object each of those inherits from, and the `node:` namespaces. A namespace inside a namespace (`path.posix`) is measured as one member, not walked.
- **Symbol-keyed members only where they are well known.** A symbol is reported as `@@name` when it is a property of `Symbol`; a registered or private symbol has no spelling this probe can put to the other engine.
- **125 receivers cannot describe themselves natively.** `Object.getOwnPropertyNames` answers on the built-ins ADR 0043 put on the shared table, on the prototypes and on the namespaces, and refuses elsewhere. On those receivers an *invented* member --- one native publishes and the interpreter does not --- is invisible, because nothing would have thought to ask for it by name.
- **Instances are not probed.** Everything here is read off a constructor, a prototype or a namespace. A member that resolves differently on a live receiver than on the prototype it came from is not something this method can reach.
